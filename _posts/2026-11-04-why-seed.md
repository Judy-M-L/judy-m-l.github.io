I did the IB diploma in Victoria, BC, then Alberta, then Ontario. Through all of that moving I kept thinking: it would be nice to have a mentor, or just someone a year ahead of me. Getting into university didn't solve anything. I signed up for every English-exchange program and college networking event the school offered just to make friends. When being alone got too heavy I went to counselling, or registered for whatever student-support program was open.

I had a lot of career questions and didn't even know the word "mentor." So every term I emailed professors and showed up to office hours. When I finally got a mentor, I had so many questions I didn't know which one to ask. Tax-filing workshops, legal-registration help, visa-extension seminars — I found those on my own, late. Last summer at Yonsei I missed a document and a warning and paid for it.

Scholarships, exchange, co-op: I learned those existed after the deadlines. And it wasn't just those three. It was, honestly, a ridiculous number of things, all learned late.

From the outside I looked fine. Inside, it had worn me down.

## So, SEED

I'm standing in front of the big door now — job hunting — and once I walk through it I won't meet many students in my situation anymore. Before that, I want to hand over everything: the mistakes, the failures, the wandering, the recovering, how to hold on. I want to pour everything I have into their success.

That's the honest version. The organisational version is: SEED Toronto is a hub where Korean, Korean-Canadian and international students in Canada can find every official benefit they're entitled to — scholarships, co-op, government programs, 208 institutions and 125 programs so far — and where they can talk to each other about how it actually went.

Who it's for: the first-year who wants to start strong, the second-year who just woke up, the third-year who says "I still have a year," the fourth-year giving it one last push. The person who thinks every worry is theirs alone and everyone else is ahead. I want to spoon-feed those people the opportunities Canada and their school already offer. Make a friend, prepare early.

And something I wrote to my team early on: I want this to be a safe zone where Koreans don't resent each other. Where you learn from the one ahead and pull along the one behind, instead of envy. Where if you don't know what you want, we help you do *something*. Where people talk honestly — relationships, grades, FOMO, all of it.

## Why the name

A seed gets planted and grows into something you can't predict. That's what I want for the students who come through.

## What I didn't expect

I thought I was building a website. What I actually ended up doing was writing a privacy policy, deciding who should be allowed to see members' birth dates, taking back a title I'd handed out too early, and learning to say no to a sponsor without losing the relationship. The website was the easy part.

I also wanted to prove something to myself: that I have the makings of a leader, and that I'm willing to study it and practise it, not just assume it. I'm still finding out.
