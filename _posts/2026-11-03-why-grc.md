I'm a supply chain management student. For most of the past year I thought I was learning operations: how to run a bakery, how to launch a student platform, how to keep an event budget from going negative.

Then I looked at what I'd actually written down.

For SEED Toronto, my student platform, I had a privacy policy with a retention table, an access-control policy with quarterly reviews, an incident response plan, and a rulebook that says the Head of Finance has to approve my own expenses. For SOBOK, my bakery, I had a list of ten regulations I had to satisfy before selling, and a decision log where I'd postponed a sale because the kitchen inspection and insurance weren't done yet.

None of that is operations. It's governance, risk and compliance. I just hadn't heard the term.

## What GRC is, in my words

Security has two halves. One half builds and breaks: engineers, pentesters, people who think in code. The other half decides what the rules are, measures what could go wrong, and checks whether the rules are actually followed. That second half is GRC.

It needs someone who understands how a business runs, can read a regulation without panicking, and will say "we're not ready" when everyone wants to ship. That's a much better description of me than "developer."

## Why not just go technical

I thought about it. I built a whole site with AI coding tools; I could keep going. But I'd be starting four years behind people who've written code since first year, competing for the same jobs they want. In GRC, my supply chain degree isn't a gap to explain. Third-party and supply chain risk is one of the fastest-growing parts of the field, and it's literally my major.

## What I'm doing about it

- **CompTIA Security+**, exam planned for January 2027. Twelve weeks, one hour a day.
- **Writing here** as I study. If I can't explain a concept using my bakery or my platform as the example, I don't understand it yet.
- **Auditing my own two organisations** against what I learn. SEED and SOBOK both have gaps I already know about (no retention policy on the bakery's order form, for one). Fixing them is my first portfolio.

I don't know yet if this is the right call. But the last two times I made a decision like this, I ended up with a platform and a bakery. So I'll keep the log going and see.
